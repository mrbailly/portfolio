Case study 01 · Lifecycle UX · Incentive messaging

Welcome flow and incentive messaging

Rewriting the first five emails new clients receive from a European online broker, including how a welcome voucher that covers transaction costs is presented.

My role
Wrote all five emails. Shaped how the voucher is presented.
Worked with
Design, Data, Legal, Compliance and the Data Protection Office
Languages
English source copy for Dutch, French, German, Italian, Portuguese and Spanish versions
Status
Approved and live

The problem

The old welcome flow tried to tell new clients everything at once. The emails were dense, hard to scan and not very user-friendly, and engagement had room to improve.

The flow also carries a welcome voucher that covers transaction costs. An incentive like that only works if people notice it, understand the conditions and know how to use it.

What I did

I wrote all five emails. For the voucher, I worked on how prominent it is, when it appears in the journey, the wording of the call to action and how the conditions are explained.

Process

  1. Follow the client's journey. I organised the flow around what a new client does next (funding the account, placing a first order, finding other products) and matched each email to the questions they have at that stage.
  2. One job per email. The old first email introduced several topics at once, such as products and trading. Now each email has one main message and one main action, and the first one focuses on the most important step: making a first deposit.
  3. Put the incentive where it's useful. The voucher is presented with its benefit and its condition together, and the call to action names what the client actually needs to do.
  4. Review with Legal, Compliance and Data Protection. Several review rounds made the copy more compliant. My job was to keep it plain and readable through each round.
  5. Write for translation. I wrote the English source so it translates cleanly and gave colleagues the context they needed for the language versions. I didn't translate the versions myself.
  6. Launch and measure. Data tracks engagement per email against the old flow.

How the decisions look in practice

The examples below are written for this portfolio to show my approach. They are not production copy, and the amounts and dates are placeholders.

How I worked with each team

  • Legal, Compliance and Data Protection

    Need copy that is accurate and compliant.

    I put the copy in a Word document and tag them for review in Jira, so they can comment line by line. If something blocks us, we sort it out in a quick Teams chat.

  • Data

    Need clear metrics to measure against.

    At kick-off I asked which metrics we'd track: open rate, click-through rate, activation and deposit rate. After launch I asked for regular updates on each.

  • Design

    Need copy that fits the layout.

    I write the first draft straight into Figma, so I know it works in the design before anyone reviews it.

  • Language colleagues

    Need a good starting point and context.

    I send a Word file per language with a version prepared by our AI workflow. They check that it's accurate and fits their culture, then approve it.

Real example · from another email I wrote

In an email about the tax implications of withdrawing pension savings early, I'd written that clients could get "tax benefits". Compliance pointed out that we had to name those benefits explicitly. I added them to the email and kept the same friendly, plain tone as the rest of it.

Result

Click-to-open rate
64% → 73%+9 percentage points

The figures reflect the redesign as a whole, so I can't attribute them to one change, such as the voucher copy, on its own.

What I learned

The most useful test for every paragraph was a simple one: does this help the client with this step? If it didn't, it moved to a later email or went.

Case study 02 · Content strategy · Stakeholder influence

A homepage built around one question

Turning a crowded broker homepage into a page that answers what a first-time visitor actually wants to know: why should I choose you?

My role
Started the project. Led content and messaging.
Worked with
Our designer, external developers and senior leadership
Approach
Benefit-led copy and a clear page hierarchy
Status
Approved and live

The problem

The homepage tried to cover too much. Much of it was information a potential client didn't need on a first visit, and that made it hard to see the one thing that mattered: why choose this broker?

Leadership's starting preference was a detailed, information-rich page. Changing that meant changing minds, not just copy.

What I did

After the welcome flow revamp, I proposed giving the homepage the same treatment. I led the content and messaging and worked with our designer on the mock-ups that made the case.

Process

  1. Agree on one question. Everything on the page had to help a visitor answer "Why should I use this broker?" or help them take the next step.
  2. Cut, then order. I took out content first-time visitors didn't need and put the rest in the order they're likely to ask about it: value, costs, trust, products, learning, action.
  3. Lead with benefits. Headlines say what the visitor gets. Features and details support them underneath.
  4. Show, don't argue. With our designer, I built mock-ups of the simpler page with the new messaging. I set up a meeting with all stakeholders, presented my reasoning and showed the mock-ups in Figma.

How I worked with each team

  • Leadership

    Started out preferring a detailed, information-rich page.

    I set up one meeting with all stakeholders, presented the case for a simpler page and showed it in Figma.

  • Design

    Need copy that works in the layout.

    We built the mock-ups together. I wrote the copy directly in Figma, so text and design developed side by side.

  • External developers

    Need a clear, final handover.

    I hand over through Jira tickets and check the build on the test site before it goes live.

Result

Average session time
11 s → 25 smeaningful sessions only
"Open an account" clicks
+12 ptsclick-through rate, in percentage points, so far

Leadership approved the simpler direction after seeing the mock-ups. The cleaner design and stronger messaging convinced them. The new homepage is live, and these are early figures compared with the old page.

What I learned

Much of content design is helping a team agree on what to leave out. A concrete mock-up made that conversation much easier than a document would have.

Case study 03 · AI · Localisation

An AI workflow for localisation

Setting up AI projects that turn approved source copy into near-final versions for every language we publish in, so colleagues review instead of translating from scratch.

My role
Set up the workflow and keep it up to date
Tools
Dedicated projects in AI tools
Languages
Mainly English and Dutch, plus every other language we publish in
Status
In use

The problem

Most of what I write goes out in several languages. Each version used to be translated from scratch by a colleague, which took time for every language and every piece of content.

What I did

I set up dedicated projects in AI tools and gave them what a good translator at the company would need to know: our writing guidelines, word lists, required disclaimers and previously approved content. I update that material regularly, so the output keeps up with how we write.

A person who speaks the language still approves every version before it's published. The AI prepares the draft; it doesn't sign it off.

Result

Colleagues now receive versions that are close to ready to ship, so their job is to check and approve them, not to translate. I haven't measured the time saved in hours, so I don't put a number on it here.

What I learned

The output is only as good as the reference material behind it. Keeping guidelines, word lists and approved examples up to date matters more than any single prompt.

How I work

Write for the whole journey

A message only makes sense next to the ones before and after it. I plan copy across emails, screens and pages, so the story stays consistent.

Frame rewards honestly

People act on incentives they understand and trust. I keep the condition next to the benefit and avoid false urgency.

Write for translation

I write source copy that travels: short sentences, consistent terms, no idioms. I give language specialists the context behind every string.

Measure, then iterate

I use engagement data, testing and feedback to decide what to change next, and I'm careful about what the numbers can and can't prove.
